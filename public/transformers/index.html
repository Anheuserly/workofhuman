02. Fundamental Working Principle

Electromagnetic induction without any physical moving parts (Static AC Machine)

Faraday & Lenz

Law of Mutual Induction

When alternating voltage $V_1$ is applied to the primary winding, it produces an alternating magnetizing current that establishes a mutual alternating flux $\Phi$ in the ferromagnetic core.

This common flux links with both the primary and secondary windings, inducing an electromotive force (EMF) in each winding according to Faraday's law:

Faraday's & Lenz's Law
e = -N \frac{d\Phi}{dt}
Negative sign indicates induced EMF opposes the cause producing it (Lenz's Law).

Ideal vs. Practical Transformer

  • Zero Winding Resistance ($R_1 = R_2 = 0$): No copper ($I^2R$) losses.
  • Zero Leakage Flux ($\Phi_{l1} = \Phi_{l2} = 0$): 100% of flux is confined in the core.
  • Infinite Permeability ($\mu_r \to \infty$): Zero reluctance; infinitesimal current required to establish flux.
  • Zero Core Losses ($P_h = P_e = 0$): No hysteresis or eddy current dissipation.
Frequency Invariance Principle
f_{primary} = f_{secondary} = f_{core}
A transformer transforms voltage and current, but NEVER alters electrical frequency.

03. Governing Equations of Transformation

Turn ratio, EMF derivation, and impedance reflection

Core Formulas
The Master EMF Equation
E_{rms} = 4.44 \cdot f \cdot N \cdot \Phi_m
E_{rms} = 4.44 \cdot f \cdot N \cdot B_m \cdot A_{core}
Where $f$ = Supply Frequency (Hz), $N$ = Number of turns, $\Phi_m$ = Peak magnetic flux (Weber), $B_m$ = Maximum flux density ($\text{Tesla}$ or $\text{Wb/m}^2$), $A$ = Net core cross-sectional area ($\text{m}^2$).
Transformation & Turns Ratio ($k$)
\frac{V_2}{V_1} \approx \frac{E_2}{E_1} = \frac{N_2}{N_1} = \frac{I_1}{I_2} = k
If $k > 1 \implies$ Step-Up; If $k < 1 \implies$ Step-Down. Note current transforms inversely to voltage.
Turns Ratio ($a$) & Impedance Reflection
a = \frac{N_1}{N_2} = \frac{1}{k}
Impedance transferred from Secondary to Primary:
R_2' = a^2 \cdot R_2 = \left(\frac{N_1}{N_2}\right)^2 R_2
X_2' = a^2 \cdot X_2 = \left(\frac{N_1}{N_2}\right)^2 X_2
Z_2' = a^2 \cdot Z_2
EMF Per Turn (Universal Invariant)
\frac{E_1}{N_1} = \frac{E_2}{N_2} = 4.44 \cdot f \cdot \Phi_m
Both windings always experience the exact same induced voltage per turn.

04. Comprehensive Transformer Taxonomy

Categorization by application, structural construction, phase count, and insulation

Classification
By Application

Grid Purpose

  • Power: High voltage transmission (>200 MVA).
  • Distribution: End-user supply (≤2500 kVA, 11kV/415V).
  • Autotransformer: Inter-grid voltage tie.
  • Instrument: CT & PT metering/protection.
  • Isolation: 1:1 safety & harmonic decoupling.
By Construction

Magnetic Design

  • Core Type: Windings encircle core limbs; preferred for high voltage.
  • Shell Type: Core encircles windings; excellent mechanical bracing.
  • Berry Type: Multi-limb radial shell; high cooling surface.
  • Toroidal: Doughnut core; near-zero leakage flux.
By Cooling Medium

Thermal Class

  • Dry-Type (VPI/Cast Resin): Fire-safe for indoor commercial facilities.
  • Oil-Immersed (ONAN/ONAF): Mineral or synthetic ester oil for high thermal dissipation.
  • Forced Cooling (OFAF/ODAF): Pumped oil & fans for massive power units.
By Phase & Freq

System Service

  • Single-Phase (1-Φ): Residential pole-top, traction.
  • Three-Phase (3-Φ): Standard commercial & grid power.
  • High-Frequency (Ferrite): SMPS, flyback, inverter stages (kHz to MHz).
  • Earthing/Grounding: Zig-zag winding creates neutral.

05. Core Construction & Magnetic Properties

Metallurgy, silicon steel laminations, and suppression of parasitic losses

Metallurgy & Physics

Cold Rolled Grain Oriented (CRGO) Steel

Modern transformer cores use silicon alloyed steel (approx 3.2% Si) cold-rolled to align crystal grains in the rolling direction, yielding very high permeability $\mu_r$ and narrow hysteresis loop.

  • Lamination Thickness: Typically 0.23 mm to 0.35 mm insulated with Carlite varnish.
  • Saturation Flux Density ($B_{sat}$): Operates between 1.6 T and 1.8 T to prevent saturation under overvoltage.
  • Step-Lap Mitred Joints: 45° mitered overlapping joints reduce joint reluctance and inrush acoustics.

Amorphous Metal (Metglas) Cores

Produced by ultra-rapid cooling (1,000,000 °C/sec) of molten alloy, resulting in non-crystalline glass-like atomic structure.

  • No-Load Loss Reduction: Reduces core hysteresis & eddy losses by up to 70% – 80% compared to standard CRGO.
  • Trade-Offs: Lower saturation flux density ($B_{sat} \approx 1.56\text{ T}$), mechanically brittle, higher initial cost.
  • Target Application: High-efficiency green distribution transformers.
Stacking Factor ($k_i$)
A_{net} = k_i \cdot A_{gross} \quad (k_i \approx 0.90 \text{ to } 0.96)
Accounts for insulation varnish occupying physical core volume.

06. Windings & Dielectric Insulation Systems

Conductors, winding geometries, and thermal classification standards

Insulation & Thermal

Winding Types & Placement

Low-voltage (LV) winding is placed nearest to the core limb to reduce required dielectric clearance to ground. High-voltage (HV) winding surrounds the LV winding.

  • Cylindrical / Layer Winding: Low voltage, up to 33 kV.
  • Helical Winding: High current LV windings (interleaved multi-strip).
  • Continuous Disc Winding: Standard for medium & high voltage systems; excellent axial short-circuit withstand.
  • Foil Winding: Copper or aluminum sheet across full axial length, ideal for Cast Resin Dry Transformers.

Thermal Insulation Classes (IEC / NEMA)

Class Max Temp Typical Materials
Class A 105 °C Kraft paper, pressboard in mineral oil.
Class B 130 °C Inorganic materials with bonding varnish.
Class F 155 °C Resin-encapsulated glass cloth, mica.
Class H 180 °C Silicone elastomers, Nomex aramid paper.
Class C > 180 °C Porcelain, ceramic, high-temp composites.

07. Power vs. Distribution Transformers

Operating philosophy, load cycles, and efficiency optimization points

Grid Engineering
Design Feature Power Transformer Distribution Transformer
Grid Location Generating stations & transmission substations (>132 kV). Distribution networks, commercial facilities, end-users (11kV/415V).
Load Profile Continuous near 100% full load around the clock. Widely fluctuating load (average 50% to 70% load over 24 hours).
Efficiency Target Engineered for Maximum Commercial Efficiency at 100% Full Load ($P_i = P_{cu,FL}$). Engineered for Maximum All-Day Efficiency at 50% – 70% Load ($P_i \ll P_{cu,FL}$).
Impedance (%Z) High (10% to 15%) to limit catastrophic downstream fault levels. Low (4% to 5%) to maintain tight voltage regulation at consumer taps.
All-Day Energy Efficiency ($\eta_{all-day}$)
\eta_{all-day} = \frac{\text{Energy Output (kWh in 24 hours)}}{\text{Energy Input (kWh in 24 hours)}} = \frac{\text{Output kWh}}{\text{Output kWh} + 24 \cdot P_i + \sum (x_t^2 P_{cu} \cdot t)} \times 100\%
Core loss ($P_i$) runs 24/7 regardless of load; copper loss scales with square of hourly load factor ($x_t^2$).

08. Autotransformers & Specialty Units

Common winding configurations, copper economy, and isolation decoupling

Specialty Machinery

Autotransformer: Principle & Economy

Utilizes a single continuous winding shared between primary and secondary, where energy is transferred partly by conduction and partly by induction.

Copper Weight Saving
\text{Weight of Cu}_{Auto} = (1 - k) \cdot \text{Weight of Cu}_{2-Winding}
Maximum economy when $k \to 1$ (e.g. stepping 400 kV to 220 kV).
Power Conduction vs Induction
S_{transformed} = (1 - k) S_{total} \quad \Big| \quad S_{conducted} = k \cdot S_{total}

Specialty Transformers

  • Galvanic Isolation Transformer (1:1 Ratio): Zero physical connection between primary and secondary. Decouples ground loops, eliminates common-mode surges, protects sensitive hospital/medical equipment.
  • Toroidal Transformer: Circular donut core with no air gap. Virtually zero acoustic hum, extremely low stray magnetic field (ideal for audio amplifiers and precision lab instrumentation).
  • Grounding / Earthing Transformer (Zig-Zag): Provides a stable artificial neutral for ungrounded Delta networks to drain fault currents without circulating zero-sequence harmonics.

09. Instrument Transformers: CT & PT

Precision measurement, relay protection, and the critical secondary open-circuit rule

Protection & Metering
Current Transformer (CT)

Series Measuring Instrument

Steps down massive primary currents (e.g. 1000A) to standard secondary values (1A or 5A) for protective relays and energy meters.

CRITICAL SAFETY RULE:

NEVER open-circuit a live CT secondary! With zero secondary counter-MMF, total primary MMF saturates the core, inducing lethal kilovolts across terminals and causing explosive dielectric failure.

Potential Transformer (PT)

Parallel Voltage Instrument

Steps down high distribution/transmission voltages (e.g. 11kV, 33kV) to safe standardized secondary levels (typically 110V or 110V/√3).

  • Burden Rating: Apparent power (VA) drawn by connected relays/meters.
  • Accuracy Classes:
    • Class 0.2S / 0.5: Revenue billing meters.
    • Class 5P10 / 5P20: Protective relaying (5% composite error up to 10x or 20x rated current).

11. Equivalent Circuit & Fault Impedance

Mathematical parameter referral to primary winding & short circuit calculation

Circuit Analysis

Exact Equivalent Circuit Parameters

  • Series Branch: Winding resistance ($R_1, R_2$) and leakage reactance ($X_1, X_2$).
  • Parallel Shunt Branch: Core-loss conductance $R_c$ (iron losses) and magnetizing susceptance $X_m$ (creates flux $\Phi_m$).
Referred to Primary Side ($a = N_1/N_2$)
R_{01} = R_1 + R_2' = R_1 + a^2 R_2
X_{01} = X_1 + X_2' = X_1 + a^2 X_2
Z_{01} = \sqrt{R_{01}^2 + X_{01}^2}

Percentage Impedance (%Z) & Fault Current

The percentage of rated primary voltage required to circulate full-load rated current under short circuit.

Percentage Impedance (%Z)
\%Z = \frac{I_{FL} \cdot Z_{01}}{V_1} \times 100\%
Short Circuit Symmetrical Fault Current ($I_{sc}$)
I_{sc} = \frac{I_{FL}}{\%Z / 100} = I_{FL} \times \left(\frac{100}{\%Z}\right)
If $\%Z = 5\%$, short circuit current is 20x rated full-load current!

12. Losses, Efficiency & Maximum Derivation

Iron vs copper losses and mathematical condition for peak efficiency

Efficiency Analysis

Loss Components

1. Core Loss (Constant with load)
P_i = P_{hysteresis} + P_{eddy}
Hysteresis: $P_h = k_h f B_m^{1.6}$ | Eddy: $P_e = k_e f^2 B_m^2 t^2$
2. Copper Loss (Variable with load factor $x$)
P_{cu}(x) = x^2 \cdot P_{cu,FL} = x^2 \cdot I_{2,FL}^2 R_{02}
At 50% load ($x = 0.5$), copper losses drop to 25% of full load!

Efficiency Formulation & Peak Condition

Commercial Efficiency Equation
\eta = \frac{x \cdot S \cdot \cos\phi}{x \cdot S \cdot \cos\phi + P_i + x^2 P_{cu,FL}} \times 100\%
Where $S$ = Rated kVA, $\cos\phi$ = Load power factor.
Condition for Maximum Efficiency ($\frac{d\eta}{dx} = 0$)
\text{Variable Copper Loss} = \text{Constant Iron Loss}
x^2 \cdot P_{cu,FL} = P_i \implies x = \sqrt{\frac{P_i}{P_{cu,FL}}}
Load for max $\eta$: $\text{kVA}_{\max\eta} = \text{Rated kVA} \times \sqrt{\frac{P_i}{P_{cu,FL}}}$

13. Voltage Regulation & Testing Procedures

Formulas for regulation and standard OC/SC bench testing

Bench Testing & VR

Voltage Regulation (VR)

The fractional change in secondary terminal voltage from no-load to full-load expressed as percentage of full-load voltage.

Approximate Regulation Equation
VR \approx \frac{I_2 (R_{02}\cos\phi \pm X_{02}\sin\phi)}{V_{2,FL}} \times 100\%
+ sign for Lagging PF (Inductive load $\implies$ voltage drops)
– sign for Leading PF (Capacitive load $\implies$ voltage rises - Ferranti effect)
Zero Voltage Regulation Condition (Leading PF)
\tan\phi = -\frac{R_{02}}{X_{02}} \implies \cos\phi = \frac{X_{02}}{Z_{02}} \text{ (leading)}

Factory Testing Benchmarks

  • Open-Circuit (OC) Test: Conducted on Low-Voltage (LV) side at rated voltage with HV side open. Wattmeter measures pure Iron Losses ($P_i$). Determines shunt parameters: $R_c$ and $X_m$.
  • Short-Circuit (SC) Test: Conducted on High-Voltage (HV) side at rated current with LV side shorted. Voltage applied is only 5% to 10% of rated $V$. Wattmeter measures pure Full-Load Copper Losses ($P_{cu,FL}$). Determines series parameters: $R_{01}$, $X_{01}$, and $Z_{01}$.
